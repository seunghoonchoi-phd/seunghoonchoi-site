![Suction-type microstructured patch conforming to skin and driving a topical agent into the stratum corneum](/images/biomimetic-suction-patch.jpg)

A co-first-authored paper published in the Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology. Octopus-inspired suction microstructures patterned into the patch hold it against the skin and help a topical agent penetrate without damaging the stratum corneum. Ten healthy women aged 31 to 63 took part in a four-week split-face study: one side of the face received both the ampule and the patch, the other side the ampule alone. At week 2, the patch side improved more on every measure — wrinkle depth fell 10.74% against 6.27% on the control side, elasticity rose 4.11% against 2.03%, and hydration rose 28.63% against 11.24%. At week 4, pigmentation fell 15.77% on the patch side against 4.99% on the control side. The differences between the two sides were statistically significant (p < 0.05), and no adverse events were observed. With 10 subjects, this is a pilot study and the sample size is small.
